# Debarment of Firms from Bidding — DoE Guidelines

> Source: Office Memorandum No. F.1/20/2018-PPD dated 02.11.2021, "Guidelines on
> Debarment of firms from Bidding", Government of India, Ministry of Finance,
> Department of Expenditure, Procurement Policy Division. Listed on the official
> Department of Expenditure website (Archive What's New, Sr. No. 92).
> The OM's detailed operative paragraphs are not reproduced here. The statutory basis
> quoted below is Rule 151 of the General Financial Rules, 2017, which the OM reproduces
> at its start ("draws attention towards Rule 151 of GFRs, 2017"). Rule 151 text is
> verbatim from the official GFR 2017 compilation.

## Statutory basis — GFR 2017, Rule 151 (Debarment from bidding)

(i) A bidder shall be debarred if he has been convicted of an offence — (a) under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988; or (b) the Indian Penal Code or any other law for the time being in force, for causing any loss of life or property or causing a threat to public health as part of execution of a public procurement contract.

(ii) A bidder debarred under sub-section (i) or any successor of the bidder shall not be eligible to participate in a procurement process of any procuring entity for a period not exceeding three years commencing from the date of debarment. Department of Commerce (DGS&D) will maintain such list which will also be displayed on the website of DGS&D as well as Central Public Procurement Portal.

(iii) A procuring entity may debar a bidder or any of its successors, from participating in any procurement process undertaken by it, for a period not exceeding two years, if it determines that the bidder has breached the code of integrity. The Ministry/Department will maintain such list which will also be displayed on their website.

(iv) The bidder shall not be debarred unless such bidder has been given a reasonable opportunity to represent against such debarment.

## Scope of the DoE guidelines — OM No. F.1/20/2018-PPD dated 02.11.2021

The Department of Expenditure's Procurement Policy Division issued the "Guidelines on Debarment of firms from Bidding" vide OM No. F.1/20/2018-PPD dated 02.11.2021. The OM draws attention towards Rule 151 of GFRs, 2017 and lays down the procedure for debarment of firms from bidding across procuring entities.

## Two types of debarment — as cited in official GeM tender documents

As per OM No. F.1/20/2018-PPD, Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance dated 02.11.2021 on the subject "Guidelines on debarment of firms from bidding": firms shall be debarred by their own Ministry/Department, or be universally debarred across all Ministries/Departments where such debarment is ordered by the Department of Expenditure. A firm may be debarred either by its own Ministry/Department or be universally debarred across all Ministries/Departments where such debarment is ordered by the Department of Expenditure.

(The statements in this section are cited from official Government e-Marketplace bid documents referencing the OM, not from the OM's own paragraph text.)

## Exclusion of debarred firms from participation

A bidder debarred under Rule 151(i) of the General Financial Rules, 2017, or any successor of the bidder, shall not be eligible to participate in a procurement process of any procuring entity for a period not exceeding three years commencing from the date of debarment. A procuring entity may debar a bidder for breach of the code of integrity for a period not exceeding two years from participation in procurement processes undertaken by it.
